# SAFE Comparative Experiment — Results, Replicated and Corrected

> This supersedes the first draft (preserved verbatim as
> [`walkthrough_gemini_original.md`](walkthrough_gemini_original.md)). Every number below
> comes from **12 runs** — 6 three-task holdouts × 2 seeds — rather than the single split
> the first draft used. Reproduce with `python3 experiments/compare_gemini_loss.py`.

---

## Headline

The intra-trajectory contrastive loss **works, and the effect replicates strongly** —
but the baseline it was compared against is SAFE's *weakest* configuration. Against
SAFE's *best* configuration, the contrastive loss does not win.

Unseen tasks (zero-shot), identical LSTM/MLP inputs, identical splits, mean ± sd over 12 runs:

| arm | loss / head | val_unseen AUROC | val_seen AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | SAFE-LSTM, time-weighted BCE (`use_time_weighting=False`) | 0.646 ± 0.048 | 0.670 ± 0.068 |
| B | SAFE-LSTM, as originally run (`use_time_weighting=True`) | 0.630 ± 0.037 | 0.656 ± 0.073 |
| C | **intra-trajectory contrastive** (this experiment) | **0.792 ± 0.065** | 0.868 ± 0.024 |
| D | Hide-and-Seek proper, inter + intra | 0.765 ± 0.072 | 0.889 ± 0.020 |
| E | **SAFE-MLP, hinge + `cumsum` — SAFE's published default** | **0.832 ± 0.059** | 0.892 ± 0.029 |

- **C beats A by +0.146 (6.3 standard errors).** Unambiguous. The first draft's single-split
  delta of +0.138 was essentially correct.
- **C vs E: −0.040 (1.6 se).** Not statistically separable, and nominally *behind*.

So the accurate claim is: **the contrastive loss lifts SAFE's LSTM/BCE variant up to roughly
the level of SAFE's MLP/hinge variant. It does not exceed SAFE's best setting.**

---

## What the first draft got right

Worth stating clearly, because these were checked and held:

1. **The baseline loss was faithful.** SAFE's `lstm.py` genuinely uses time-weighted BCE with
   failure as the positive class when `cumsum == False`
   ([`lstm.py:99-107`](failure_prob/model/lstm.py#L99)), and `LstmModelConfig.cumsum` really
   does default to `False` ([`conf/__init__.py:226`](failure_prob/conf/__init__.py#L226)).
   Hyperparameters (lr 3e-4, hidden 256, 1 layer) match SAFE's defaults exactly.
2. **The task split is correct.** `split_rollouts` groups by `task_id`, so it avoids a real
   trap: the 19 WidowX folders collapse to only **8 distinct tasks**
   (`task_lift_red_bottle_1..4` are all `task_id=2`). Splitting on folder names would have
   leaked the same task into train and test.
3. **`.detach()` on the argmax is necessary**, for the reason stated — otherwise gradients act
   on the *position* of the maximum difference.
4. **It is not the degenerate pure-intra objective.** The `relu(scores).mean()` term on success
   trajectories anchors the success class, doing the job the inter-trajectory term does in the
   published loss. Without it the objective would be shift-invariant and would not train.
5. **The direction and magnitude of the effect survived replication.** That is the main thing.

---

## Four claims that need correcting

### 1. The "33× post/pre ratio" is circular

The modified loss is *trained* to maximise `post_mean − pre_mean` at the `argmax`-of-differences
split point. The metric then measures that same quantity at that same split point. It restates
the objective rather than testing it. WidowX has **no failure-onset annotations**
(`failure_time_label_path` is null in `conf/dataset/openvla_widowx.yaml`), so there is no ground
truth to validate against.

The evidence that this metric is not what matters:

| arm | post/pre ratio (unseen) | val_unseen AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| A SAFE-LSTM | 1.5 | 0.646 |
| C intra-contrastive | **22.7** | 0.792 |
| D H&S inter+intra | **1.6** | 0.765 |

Arm D reaches comparable detection quality with a post/pre ratio of **1.6**. A 14× difference
in "sharpness" produces essentially no difference in detection. Recommend dropping this as the
headline claim.

### 2. There is no early-detection benefit — the entire gain is late

AUROC using only the first half of each episode, unseen tasks:

| arm | AUROC @ 50% of episode | AUROC @ full episode |
|---|---|---|
| A SAFE-LSTM | 0.595 ± 0.030 | 0.646 |
| B SAFE-LSTM as-run | 0.595 ± 0.023 | 0.630 |
| C intra-contrastive | 0.599 ± 0.030 | 0.792 |
| D H&S inter+intra | 0.602 ± 0.046 | 0.765 |

**All four arms are identical at mid-episode (~0.60).** The contrastive loss adds nothing until
the second half. This is the expected consequence of a flat-then-spike score: if the score is
designed to stay flat until onset, there is nothing to read early. Since a failure detector is
only actionable while there is still time to intervene, this is a material limitation.

### 3. The trajectory figure does not show what its caption says

![Trajectory comparison](figure_trajectory_comparison.png)

The caption describes the baseline as "a gradual smeared rise". The plotted curve does the opposite: it starts at
0.58, peaks at 1.0 around t=25, then **falls to ~0 by t=45**. That is a rise-then-collapse, not
smearing. Separately, the modified curve peaks at only **0.15**, at t≈48 of 50 — so on this
trajectory a 0.5 threshold would never fire at all, and the "spike" arrives with two timesteps
left.

Also: `pick_failure_trajectory` selects the trajectory with the **largest score range**
(`generate_figures.py:43-60`), i.e. it selects on the quantity being illustrated; and the
vertical line labelled "onset" is the model's own `argmax(diff)`, not an annotation. Fine as an
illustration, but it cannot be presented as evidence.

### 4. The PCA figure shows task structure, not outcome separation

![Latent space PCA](figure_latent_space_pca.png)

The caption says "Separation confirms the 4096-D representations carry discriminative outcome
information." The clusters are
**tasks** (6–8 blobs, matching 8 tasks), with success and failure thoroughly mixed *inside*
every blob. Measured on the same embeddings:

- variance explained by outcome (success vs failure): **2.1 %**
- PC1 + PC2 together: 28.7 % of variance, dominated by task identity
- yet a linear probe recovers outcome at **0.876** 5-fold CV accuracy

So outcome information *is* present — the conclusion is right — but it lives in a low-variance
direction that the top two principal components cannot show. The figure neither supports nor
refutes the claim. What it *does* show is worth keeping, reframed: **the representation is
organised by task, not by outcome.** That is a useful result in its own right (see below).

Minor: reporting AUROC gains as "+40 %" and balanced-accuracy gains as "+70 %" inflates them.
AUROC is floored at 0.5, so relative percentages on it are not meaningful. Use absolute deltas.

On balanced accuracy specifically — I expected the fixed 0.5 threshold to be unfairly punishing
the baseline. It is not. Recomputed at a calibrated threshold (80th percentile of `val_seen`
success scores) instead of 0.5, unseen tasks: **C 0.686 vs A 0.563**. The gap narrows from
+0.178 to +0.123 but survives. The claim holds; only the size changes.

## The result that ties this to the rest of the project

Ranking the arms by whether the score is **cumulatively aggregated**:

| aggregation | arms | val_unseen AUROC |
|---|---|---|
| `cumsum` on | E (SAFE-MLP) | **0.832** |
| per-step | C, D | 0.792, 0.765 |
| per-step + BCE | A, B | 0.646, 0.630 |

This matches an independent finding from a synthetic study in
[`experiments/MEETING_BRIEF.md`](experiments/MEETING_BRIEF.md), where the `cumsum` aggregation —
not the loss — was what drove post-onset discriminability. Two different experiments, one
synthetic and one on real rollouts, pointing the same way: **the temporal aggregation is doing
more work than the loss function.** That convergence is the most defensible claim available here.

It also explains why the original comparison looked so favourable: it compared two *per-step*
losses against each other, while SAFE's own best setting uses cumulative aggregation.

## What to tell the professor

1. **The experiment ran, on real data, and the headline effect replicates.** The contrastive
   loss beats SAFE's LSTM/BCE variant by +0.146 AUROC at 6.3 se over 12 runs. The single-split
   number in the first draft was, in fact, accurate.
2. **But it does not beat SAFE.** SAFE's published MLP/hinge/`cumsum` configuration reaches
   0.832 on the same features and splits — nominally ahead of the contrastive loss's 0.792. The
   comparison was against SAFE's weakest setting.
3. **Aggregation appears to matter more than the loss**, and this is now supported by two
   independent experiments.
4. **The sharpness metric is circular and should be dropped**; Hide-and-Seek proper gets
   comparable accuracy at a post/pre ratio of 1.6 versus 22.7.
5. **No method detects failures early.** All arms sit at ~0.60 AUROC at mid-episode. This is the
   honest limitation of the whole line of work and the most interesting open problem.
6. **The feature space is organised by task, not outcome** (2.1 % of variance is outcome). This
   is why zero-shot calibration is hard, and it connects directly to the conformal-coverage
   results in `experiments/MEETING_BRIEF.md`.

---

## Limitations of this replication

- 8 distinct tasks only; 3-task holdouts are coarse. 12 runs give spread, not tight significance.
- `last`-token features throughout (matching the original setup). Arm E's 0.832 is therefore
  strictly comparable to arms A–D, but not to the `mean`-token numbers elsewhere in the project.
- `lambda_reg` set to 0 for all arms; SAFE's LSTM default is 1.0 and its scripts sweep
  1e-3 … 1. No hyperparameter search was run for any arm, including the contrastive loss —
  which means C could plausibly improve with tuning, and so could E.
- No failure-onset annotations exist for WidowX, so onset localisation cannot be validated at
  all on this dataset — only inferred.
- 100 epochs, batch 16, single learning rate, as in the original scripts.

## Reproducing

```bash
python3 experiments/cache_widowx.py --token last     # 532 episodes -> one tensor (~4 s)
python3 experiments/compare_gemini_loss.py           # arms A-D, 12 runs (~20 min, MPS)
```

Arm E is `train_probe(..., "safe", "cumsum", ...)` from `experiments/run_real_widowx.py` on the
same folds. Raw per-run numbers: `experiments/out/gemini_compare.csv`.

# Appendix — Project overview (source material for the two-pager)

Everything below is context for building a short write-up: what was done, on what data, with
what code, and which figures carry the argument. The comparative experiment above is one of
four studies; this section places it in the whole.

## In plain language

A robot arm does short chores and sometimes fails. SAFE builds a **failure score** that watches
the policy's internal state and rises when things are going wrong, then draws a threshold using
conformal prediction — which comes with a promise: *false alarms on successful runs will happen
at most α of the time.*

Three things were found by testing that promise:

1. **The promise only holds on chores the detector was calibrated on.** On a genuinely new task
   it breaks — one detector variant raised a false alarm on essentially *every* successful run.
2. **The cause is a scale shift.** On unfamiliar tasks the score runs about **3× higher even
   when nothing goes wrong**, so the threshold sits in the wrong place. It is a thermometer that
   reads hot in every new room.
3. **Letting the threshold adapt online fixes it.** The detector keeps score of its own false
   alarms and adjusts as it goes. The promise is restored, and — more importantly — becomes
   *predictable* instead of varying wildly from task to task.

And one honest negative: **no method warns early.** Halfway through an episode every detector
tested is near chance. They only become accurate near the end, when there is no time left to
intervene.

## Headline numbers across all four studies

| finding | evidence |
|---|---|
| Conformal guarantee fails zero-shot | coverage 0.710 / 0.567 / **0.001** on unseen tasks vs 0.792 / 0.769 / 0.775 on an exchangeable control, nominal 0.80 |
| Cause is a task-dependent scale shift | successful-episode peak score **5.43 → 15.29**; 31 % of variance is between-task; outcome is only **2.1 %** of embedding variance |
| Online CP restores validity | ACI + growing pool: **0.817 / 0.797 / 0.774**; sd collapses 0.148 → 0.029 |
| Aggregation beats loss engineering | `cumsum` on 0.832 vs per-step 0.792 / 0.765 / 0.646; synthetic replication 0.593 → 0.801 from removing `cumsum` alone |
| Nothing detects early | AUROC at mid-episode **0.595 / 0.595 / 0.599 / 0.602** — statistically identical |
| Per-task spread is large | SAFE-MLP 0.69 ("Lift Red Bottle") → 0.98 ("Put the Red Block into the Pot") |
| Handcrafted signals are near chance | all 7 CSV signals 0.39–0.60 vs 0.796 learned — supports SAFE's core premise |

## Verification — worth including, it is what earns trust

- Reconstructed conformal band matches SAFE's `get_one_sided_prediction_band` to **1.1e-06**
  relative error
- ACI at γ = 0 reduces to split conformal prediction **exactly** (difference 0)
- Split CP lands on nominal coverage on the exchangeable control — the gate every other claim
  depends on
- SAFE-MLP reaches 0.796–0.832 unseen-task AUROC, confirming real signal rather than plumbing

## Figures, ranked for a short write-up

Ranked by how much argument each one carries. The first three fit a two-pager.

### 1. Conformal coverage under task shift — the single best panel

![Calibration diagram](experiments/out/fig5_calibration.png)

Nominal coverage on the x-axis, realised coverage on the y-axis; **on the dashed diagonal =
the guarantee holds.** Light blue is the exchangeable control (held-out episodes of the *same*
tasks as calibration) and sits on the diagonal. Red is SAFE's own setup on *unseen* tasks and
sits far below it — flat on **zero** for SAFE-Embed, at every α. Teal is ACI with a growing
calibration pool, back on the diagonal.

The control is what makes this a result rather than a bug report: same band, same code, same
detector. Only task identity changes.

### 2. Early detection — the honest limitation

![Early detection](experiments/out/fig8_early_detection.png)

AUROC when only the first fraction of an episode has been observed. For the first ~40 % of an
episode every method except SAFE-MLP is at or **below** chance, and at the halfway point only
about 40–50 % of the final above-chance signal exists. A failure detector is only actionable
while there is still time to intervene, so this is the regime that matters — and it is where
all of these methods are weakest.

### 3. Per-task breakdown and the free baselines

![Per-task and handcrafted](experiments/out/fig9_pertask_handcrafted.png)

**Left:** a single reported average of 0.796 spans **0.69 to 0.98** across chores, and the
pattern is interpretable — the three hardest are all *"Lift …"* tasks, the easiest are
*"Put … into/on …"* tasks. Lifting failures are subtle (the gripper closes on nothing);
placing failures are gross. **Right:** all seven training-free signals from the episode CSVs
cluster near chance (0.39–0.60) against 0.796 for the learned probe — which supports SAFE's
central premise that the policy's internal features carry information token-level uncertainty
does not.

### 4. Coverage recovering live along the deployment stream

![Rolling coverage](experiments/out/fig4_rolling_coverage.png)

The most visually persuasive of the set. Rolling coverage as episodes arrive, dotted lines
marking task boundaries. SAFE-Embed's split CP (red, middle panel) is pinned at zero for the
entire stream; ACI climbs to nominal within ~60 episodes. It also shows the honest caveat —
coverage still dips after each task boundary, so ACI fixes the average and the variance but not
the transient.

### 5. Loss × aggregation, crossed on synthetic data

![Factorial](experiments/out/fig2_factorial.png)

Why the aggregation matters more than the loss. Holding SAFE's loss fixed and only removing
`cumsum` moves post-onset AUC 0.593 → 0.801; the Hide-and-Seek loss at matched aggregation
reaches 0.792, i.e. indistinguishable. This is the synthetic counterpart to the arm-ordering
in the table above, and the two agree.

## One-sentence summary

> SAFE's conformal false-alarm guarantee holds on the tasks it calibrated on and fails on tasks
> it has not seen — which is the setting the paper is about. The cause is a 3× task-dependent
> score offset; adaptive conformal inference closes it; and along the way the temporal
> aggregation turns out to matter more than the loss function.

## Caveats to keep attached to any of these numbers

8 distinct tasks only, so task holdouts are coarse; ~200-episode deployment streams while ACI's
guarantee is asymptotic; one VLA and one embodiment; no failure-onset annotations in this
dataset, so onset localisation is only measurable on the synthetic testbed; no hyperparameter
search for any arm; and ACI assumes the episode outcome is observed after each episode — which
SAFE already assumes for training, but offline conformal prediction does not need.
